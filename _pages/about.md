I am Fares Fourati, a Ph.D. candidate in Machine Learning at [KAUST](https://www.kaust.edu.sa/en/). I work at the intersection of reinforcement learning, optimization, and large language models, with the broader aim of advancing the foundations required for [Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20784).

My research has been published in leading AI/ML/NLP venues, including ICML, AAAI, AISTATS, EMNLP, and ECAI, and I regularly serve as a reviewer for these conferences. During my doctoral studies, I received several distinctions, including two consecutive selections to the CEMSE Dean’s List at KAUST (2023/2024 and 2024/2025). I also earned first place in the ACM–SIAM Student Competition (2024), was recognized as a KAUST AI Rising Star (2023), and won first place at the Marconi Society’s Connectivity Summit (2021). Earlier, during my undergraduate studies, I became a Kaggle Competitions Expert (2020).

My academic trajectory began in Tunisia, where I ranked among the top 1% nationally in the Mathematics and Physics entrance examination for engineering schools (2017). I went on to earn the Diplôme d’Ingénieur in Multidisciplinary Engineering from the [École Polytechnique de Tunisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia_Polytechnic_School) in 2020, before completing an M.Sc. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at KAUST in 2022.

At a deeper level, my work is guided by a longstanding curiosity about how intelligence emerges, adapts, and scales—whether in humans or in artificial systems—and by the conviction that AI can reshape how we understand ourselves and the world. This perspective also inspired my book, [To What End? Meditations on AI and Humanity](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/242248267-to-what-end-meditations-on-ai-and-humanity), which explores the philosophical dimensions of AI and its role in broader patterns of human progress.
